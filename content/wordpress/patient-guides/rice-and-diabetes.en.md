# Can I eat rice if I have diabetes?

Having diabetes does not mean that every familiar food has to disappear from your plate. Rice can fit into a meal plan; the amount and the rest of your meal matter. Your plan should suit your medicines, health needs and routine. [NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/healthy-living-with-diabetes)

**Start with the whole plate**

A useful starting point is a roughly 23-centimetre dinner plate:

- Half: non-starchy vegetables.
- One quarter: protein foods.
- One quarter: carbohydrate foods, including rice.

For an everyday Andhra meal, discuss vegetables, rice and your usual protein foods with your care team. Pappu and other pulses provide carbohydrate as well as protein; they are not unlimited extras. These are local examples, and this plate is a guide rather than a fixed prescription. [CDC meal planning](https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/healthy-eating/diabetes-meal-planning.html)

**Keep meals and medicines working together**

Skipping meals can cause low blood sugar when you take insulin or certain diabetes medicines. Ask your doctor how your meals should fit your treatment and workday. [NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/healthy-living-with-diabetes)

**Bring your everyday questions**

At your next visit, describe a usual breakfast, lunch and dinner. Ask: “How much rice suits me?”, “How should I include pappu?” and “What should I do when a meal is delayed?” A plan you understand is easier to use at home.

**A word about remedies**

Discuss Ayurvedic products or supplements with your treating doctor before adding them. Avoid delaying diabetes assessment while trying a remedy. [NCCIH](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ayurvedic-medicine-in-depth)

General information for adults. It does not replace an individual meal plan or consultation.
